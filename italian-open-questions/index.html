    Open questions

    Every form LinguaMorpha produces for Italian is compared with an external source — the verbs with Wiktionary's conjugation tables. Every disagreement is recorded with its reason. Most are decided: a choice between grammars, a form the source lacks, a slip on our side that was fixed. The ones below are not. They are questions of Italian, not of software, and rather than settle them by guesswork they are listed here, for anyone who can answer them.

    The questions are published as they stand in the register, in English.

    Verbs — against Wiktionary

    Nothing open in this section.

    Decided and recorded in this section (5) — open for the reasons

    Each line is one recorded disagreement: what it concerns, the verdict the comparison gave, and the reason the register keeps — the maintainers' own note, in English.

    • imbrunire, nevicare, piovere · we suppress — Impersonal, by the shared impersonal flag that nevar, pleuvoir and pluere carry: nevica 'it snows' in the third person singular of every tense, with no command. Wiktionary lists the third person plural too (nevicano), a figurative use (things snowing down), not what a learner is taught; the first and second persons it leaves empty itself. piovere, flagged impersonal with the tense examples (2026-11-15), is the same verdict on fuller evidence: Wiktionary conjugates it in every person (piovo, piovono — the figurative *piovono critiche*); the course teaches piove, as it teaches llueve and il pleut. imbrunire ('it gets dark', è imbrunito), flagged impersonal with its tense examples (2026-11-17), is the same verdict: Treccani gives its intransitive sense of the sky and the air growing dark, with essere; Wiktionary conjugates every person.
    • compiere · DIFFERS — compiere takes compire's forms everywhere except the gerundio compiendo and the participle compiuto — Treccani: 'pass. rem. compiéi e più com. compìi, da compire; le altre forme da compire, eccetto il gerundio compiendo e il part. pass. compiuto'. So compivo, compii / compì, compirò, compirei, compissi and voi compite. Wiktionary's own table marks these compi- forms 'more common' beside compievo, compiei, compierò, compiessi and compiete; the oracle carries only the compie- ones. Proofreaders of the tense examples flagged compievo, compié and compiessero (2026-11-16); 31 cells.
    • succedere · we suppress — The course's succedere is 'to happen' (è successo), third person only with no command, by the thirdPersonOnly flag accadere carries. Wiktionary conjugates the verb in every person because it also means 'to succeed, to follow' (succedere al trono, participle succeduto), a sense the course does not teach.
    • dispiacere, dolere, piacere … (4) — imperativo* · we suppress — Verbs the catalogue declares to have no imperative, as Spanish's gustar, doler and parecer are: you cannot order someone to piacere, dolere or sembrare. A semantic claim rather than a morphological one, which is why the oracle disagrees — the forms are formable and Wiktionary forms them. Listed by lemma so that a new verb needs a decision.
    • restringere, restringersi — __participle__ · DIFFERS — ristretto, not restretto. Treccani and Zingarelli give ristretto as the participle of restringere (as of stringere → stretto with the ri- of the noun ristretto); restretto is a rare variant.

    If you can answer one of these, write to us through the Support page — a reference to a grammar or a text is enough. An answer changes the register, the register changes the app, and this page shortens.
